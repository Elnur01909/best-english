## VOCABULARY — Section A

1. **ABSENT / ABSENCE**  
   If you are absent you are not in the place you should be (e.g., not at work due to illness). An 'absence' is a single period of time during which a person is absent.

2. **ACCRUE / ACCRUED / ACCRUAL**  
   To accumulate over time. In employment contracts, used for rights (e.g., paid holiday) that have built up over a period.

3. **ADMINISTRATIVE WORK / DUTIES**  
   Office work carried out by secretaries and managers in support of a business. Connected with the 'paperwork' rather than the main activity.

4. **ADVERSE EFFECT**  
   A negative or unwanted effect upon something. *"Cigarette smoke has an adverse effect upon the health of employees."*

5. **AFFECT / EFFECT**  
   Common confusion: 'affect' is a verb; 'effect' is a noun. *"The new law will seriously affect many employers. One major effect will be an increase in claims."*

6. **AGE DISCRIMINATION**  
   Treating a person less favourably because of their age. Illegal in the EU at the interview stage and during employment. Even wording such as 'energetic staff required' has been interpreted as discrimination against older people.

7. **ALLEGATION**  
   A statement that something has happened, similar to an accusation. The truth has not yet been proved.

8. **APPEAL**  
   To make a formal request for a decision to be changed. When an employee is dismissed, they must be given reasons in writing and allowed to appeal.

9. **APPLICANT(S)**  
   A person who has applied for a job. Also known as a 'candidate'.

10. **APPOINTED / APPOINTMENT**  
    'Appointment' describes a job role after a suitable candidate has accepted and commenced employment.

11. **APPRAISAL**  
    A meeting between an employee and manager during which the employee's performance is assessed and discussed. Usually held annually.

12. **ASSESSMENT OF PERFORMANCE**  
    Common during a probationary period — the employer assesses whether to offer a longer contract. Terms during probation are usually less favourable than permanent contract terms.

13. **ATTRIBUTABLE TO**  
    Means 'is the cause of' or 'is the reason for'. *"His absences were attributable to his serious illness."*

14. **AUTOMATICALLY UNFAIR**  
    An employer's action that employment law does not allow to be considered fair under any circumstances. Dismissing a woman because she is pregnant is 'automatically unfair'.

## VOCABULARY — Section B

1. **BACK PAY / BACK TAXES**  
   'Back pay' is money owed to an employee that was not paid on time. 'Back taxes' are taxes that should have been paid in the past but were not.

2. **BACKGROUND CHECK**  
   A check on a person before they start work. Includes checking for criminal convictions. Required for police work or working with children.

3. **BANK HOLIDAYS**  
   Days upon which a bank is legally closed. Includes normal public holidays (Christmas, New Year) and unexpected ones (e.g., a royal wedding).

4. **BANK TRANSFER**  
   Method of salary payment using the employee's account number and sort code.

5. **BANKRUPTCY**  
   The legal situation of a person not having enough money to pay their debts. May exclude people from certain government jobs. Application forms often ask whether a person is currently bankrupt.

6. **BEAR / BORNE**  
   To accept responsibility for or pay for something. *"All travel costs will be borne by the employer."*

7. **BENEFIT(S)**  
   (1) Money paid by the government to those not working ('unemployment benefit'). (2) Any payment or advantage in an employment context.

8. **BENEFITS IN KIND**  
   Non-cash benefits such as a company car or private medical insurance. Informally called 'fringe benefits' or 'perks of the job'.

9. **BONUS PAYMENT**  
   Additional payment beyond contractual pay, related to targets or a specific occasion (e.g., Christmas bonus).

10. **BOUND BY**  
    Having a legal obligation to keep a promise or comply with terms.

11. **BRANCH**  
    A particular office or place of work that is part of a larger network.

12. **BREACH OF CONTRACT**  
    Failure to fulfil your obligations under a contract. The correct way to express 'breaking' a contract.

13. **BULLYING OR INTIMIDATORY CONDUCT**  
    Behaviour that makes an employee feel frightened, unhappy or uncomfortable. Employers have a legal duty to prevent bullying in the workplace.

14. **BUSINESS DAY**  
    A normal working day (Monday–Friday, 9am–5pm), excluding weekends and public/bank holidays.

## VOCABULARY — Section C (Employment)

1. **CAPABILITY**  
   The ability of an employee to do their job. 'Capability' dismissal occurs when an employee is genuinely unable to do their job due to illness or lack of skill.

2. **CASUAL WORKER**  
   A worker without a fixed contract who is hired as and when needed. Also known as an 'irregular worker' or 'zero-hours worker'.

3. **CLAIMANT**  
   In employment law, the person who brings a claim to an Employment Tribunal — usually the employee.

4. **COLLECTIVE AGREEMENT**  
   An agreement negotiated between an employer and a trade union on behalf of employees, covering wages, hours and working conditions.

5. **COMMISSION**  
   Payment calculated as a percentage of the value of sales made by an employee. Common in sales roles.

6. **COMPENSATION**  
   Money paid to an employee to make up for a loss suffered. In employment law, often awarded by a tribunal after unfair dismissal.

7. **COMPROMISE AGREEMENT**  
   A legally binding agreement between employer and employee to settle a dispute, usually including a payment and agreement not to bring future claims. Now called a 'Settlement Agreement' in UK law.

8. **CONFIDENTIALITY CLAUSE**  
   A clause preventing employees from disclosing confidential business information to competitors or the public. Also called a 'non-disclosure agreement' (NDA).

9. **CONSTRUCTIVE DISMISSAL**  
   When an employee resigns because the employer's behaviour has made continued employment impossible. The employee can claim they were effectively 'dismissed'.

10. **CONTRACT OF EMPLOYMENT**  
    The legal agreement between employer and employee setting out the terms and conditions of employment.
