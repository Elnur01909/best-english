## CASE 1: THE SALMON FARM

### Background
In 2005, a small ship called **The Anglian Sovereign** (owned by Klyne Marine Services) was contracted by the British Marine and Coastguard Agency (MCA) to patrol near the Shetland Islands, Scotland. On 3 September 2005, the ship hit rocks near the uninhabited island of Oxna, fracturing its fuel tank and spilling 84 tonnes of diesel fuel into the sea.

The captain, Peter Leask, was found to be three times over the legal alcohol limit and was later imprisoned for eight months (March 2006).

### Legal Action
Local salmon farming business **Hjaltland Sea Farms** brought a civil action against Klyne Marine Services for:
- Loss of profit (stopped work at three fish farms for three days)
- Extra management time to deal with the crisis
- Cost of buying cleaning equipment

### Outcome
**Lerwick Sheriff Court (July 2010):** Judge Sheriff Graeme Napier awarded the claimant **£25,120.71** (£24,020.71 for loss of profit) plus 8% interest over five years. Klyne Marine Services appealed but later withdrew.

### Legal Significance
Sets a **precedent**: anyone who pollutes the sea must accept that it is entirely **foreseeable** that the fish farm industry will suffer loss, and where there is foreseeable loss, the injured party is **entitled to receive damages**.

### Key Legal Terms
`claimant` · `defendant` · `civil action` · `damages` · `foreseeable loss` · `precedent` · `loss of profit` · `appeal`

## CASE 2: THE MOVIE STUDIO

### Background
In September 2008, a case came before a court in Delhi, India. **Warner Brothers** (USA) sued **Mirchi Movies** (India) for infringement of intellectual property rights.

Mirchi Movies had made a new film called **'Hari Puttar'** — about a ten-year-old Indian boy who comes to England and saves the world. Warner Brothers argued that 'Hari Puttar' was too similar to 'Harry Potter' and would cause public confusion.

### Outcome
The judge **found in favour of the defendant**. Reasons:
- Readers of J.K. Rowling's books are educated people who know the difference between 'Potter' and 'Puttar'
- 'Hari' is a very common Indian name; 'Puttar' means 'son' in Hindi
- The two stories are completely different

Warner Brothers also knew about the film's name in 2005 but waited until the film was finished to file the lawsuit, causing harm to Mirchi Movies' advertising campaign.

### Key Legal Terms
`intellectual property rights` · `infringement` · `franchise` · `claimant` · `defendant` · `lawsuit` · `filed`

9. Why did the timing of the case harm Mirchi Movies?
10. Who said he hoped the movie would make a lot of money in cinemas?

### Answer Key

## CASE 3: THE NEIGHBOURS

### Background
In November 2009, **Robert and Mrs Anne Young** (journalists from London) bought a house at 1 Oaklands, Burbridge, Bournemouth for £200,000 from **Philip and Francesca Longbottom**.

Under English land law, sellers must complete a **Seller's Property Information Form (SPIF)** disclosing disputes with neighbours. The Longbottoms stated they had no neighbour problems — but had 12 years of documented disputes with the Avilez family next door (described by the judge as "the neighbours from hell": loud music, drug dealing, 12 cats and 5 dogs).

### Outcome
The High Court awarded Mr and Mrs Young **£70,000 plus legal costs** for the Longbottoms' failure to disclose material information.

### Legal Significance
Lawyers expected many similar cases to follow.

### Key Legal Terms
`vendor` · `Seller's Property Information Form (SPIF)` · `disclose` · `damages` · `grounds` · `real property` · `High Court`

## CASE 4: THE TELECOMMUNICATIONS ENGINEER

### Background
In 2008, the Court of Appeal in London reviewed the case **Peakman v Linbrooke Services**. The original case was in Sheffield County Court. Court of Appeal judge **Lord Justice Goldring** called the case "deeply troubling" — total costs exceeded **£100,000** for a dispute involving a few thousand pounds.

**Facts:** Telecommunications company Linbrooke Services hired Mr Peakman to work on cables near a railway line for their client, The Thales Group. Linbrooke terminated the contract, calling his work 'defective'. Mr Peakman sued for **£2,232** (breach of contract, petrol costs, lost tools).

Linbrooke counterclaimed for **£3,019** (correction costs) plus approximately **£40,000** (loss of Thales Group contract).

### Outcome
Judge Robinson awarded: Mr Peakman £1,145 / Linbrooke £1,410 (net difference: £265 + interest). Total legal costs: over £100,000.

Lord Justice Goldring said the counterclaim "had no merit" and that the first judge was wrong to allow it. The case lasted nine days including the appeal.

### Key Legal Terms
`Court of Appeal` · `County Court` · `breach of contract` · `counterclaim` · `defective` · `liable` · `judgment` · `costs`
