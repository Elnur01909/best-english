## VOCABULARY — Section A

1. **ABOVEMENTIONED / AFOREMENTIONED / AFORESAID**  
   These words mean 'already mentioned in this document' or 'just mentioned'. Modern drafting advice is to avoid these words as they are 'archaic' (old-fashioned). Most law firms' drafting guides recommend not using them.

2. **ACCOUNTING PERIOD**  
   A clearly defined period of time during which one party will provide financial information to the other. Normally one year.

3. **ACCRUE / ACCRUAL**  
   To accrue means to accumulate over time. Used to describe how debt or interest builds up over a period of time. *"Interest shall accrue at 9% per annum."*

4. **ADMINISTRATION**  
   When a business goes into administration, it is in the hands of an administrator (trustee) who acts in the interests of creditors. Does not necessarily mean the business is sold — it may be rescued as a 'going concern'.

5. **ADVERSE MATERIAL BREACH**  
   A breach of contract so significant it usually leads to the right to terminate the agreement. Also called 'repudiation'. A minor breach only gives the right to claim damages.

6. **ALTERNATIVE DISPUTE RESOLUTION (ADR)**  
   An alternative to litigation. Includes arbitration, negotiation, mediation and collaborative law. Currently the most common form of ADR in the UK is mediation.

7. **ARISING OUT OF / FROM**  
   Commonly used phrase meaning 'as a result of' or 'created by'.

8. **ARREARS**  
   Money owed but not paid on the agreed date. Not the same as 'debt' — arrears is a debt that has not been paid on time. *"Interest shall accrue on arrears."*

9. **ASSIGNMENT**  
   The unconditional legal transfer of ownership of something. Permanent (unlike a licence, which is temporary).

10. **ASSIGNOR / ASSIGNEE**  
    An **assignor** transfers ownership rights to another. An **assignee** is the person who receives an assignment.

11. **ASSIGNS**  
    Contracts refer to 'assigns' — same meaning as assignees.

12. **ASSOCIATED COMPANY**  
    A company joined to or controlled by another company.

13. **AVOIDANCE OF DOUBT**  
    Used to clarify something just stated. Means 'to make absolutely clear'.

14. **AWARDED AGAINST**  
    Costs that a court has ordered one party to pay to the other.

## VOCABULARY — Section B

1. **BALANCE**  
   What remains after an amount has been paid into or withdrawn from an initial sum.

2. **BANKRUPT / BANKRUPTCY PETITION**  
   A court declaration that an individual cannot pay their debts. 'Bankrupt' is used for individuals; companies are 'insolvent' then 'in liquidation'. A bankruptcy petition is an application to the court to make a person bankrupt.

3. **BASE RATE**  
   The rate of interest used by commercial banks as a basis for lending rates. Used in payment clauses: *"interest shall accrue at 5% above Barclays Bank's base rate."*

4. **BEAR / BORNE**  
   To bear = to carry the burden of or pay for something. 'Borne' is the passive. *"All travel costs will be borne by the employer."*

5. **BEST ENDEAVOURS**  
   An obligation to use every reasonable course of action to achieve something. 'Reasonable endeavours' is a lesser obligation. Modern drafting advice is to avoid both phrases and instead state clearly what is expected.

6. **BILL OF LADING**  
   A document used when goods are transported by ship. Lists goods, states terms of carriage, and provides a statement of ownership during transit.

7. **BOARD**  
   Usually refers to a board of directors — the group of directors elected by shareholders to run a company.

8. **BOILERPLATE CLAUSES**  
   Standard or miscellaneous clauses at the end of every commercial contract (e.g., force majeure, governing law).

9. **BOUND**  
   Legally obliged to do something. Passive of 'bind'.

10. **BREACH**  
    To breach a contract = to fail to fulfil an obligation. More common in commercial contracts than 'violate' or 'infringe'.

11. **BREACHING PARTY**  
    The party that has failed to fulfil its obligations. Also called 'the party in breach'.

12. **BREAKDOWN**  
    An itemised list showing how a total was reached.

13. **BUSINESS DAY**  
    Normal working days (Monday–Friday, 9am–5pm), excluding weekends and public holidays.

14. **BY**  
    A deadline expression in contracts. Modern drafting advice: use 'on or before' to avoid ambiguity. *"Payment must be made on or before 29 April."*

## VOCABULARY — Section C

1. **CALENDAR MONTH**  
   A term used for certainty: a specific month (e.g., February, March), not simply 28 or 30 days. A calendar month can start on any date.

2. **CEASE**  
   Formal legal word meaning to stop.

3. **CHARGE**  
   A type of security for a debt. A **fixed charge** exists on a specific asset. A **floating charge** is over a group of changeable assets (e.g., stock).

4. **CLAUSE HEADINGS**  
   Descriptive headings for sections of a contract (e.g., 'INDEMNITIES', 'DELIVERY DATE'). Not usually binding — for information only.

5. **COMPLETION DATE / CLOSING**  
   The date when the main purpose of the contract takes effect (e.g., when shares are exchanged for price). 'Closing' is more common in the USA.

6. **CONDITIONAL ON / UPON**  
   Means something will only happen if something else happens first. Same as 'subject to' and 'provided that'.

7. **CONDITIONS PRECEDENT**  
   Pre-contractual conditions that must be met before the contract becomes binding or before one party can demand performance.

8. **CONFLICT OF INTEREST**  
   When a decision-maker can profit from their position due to connections with certain people or organisations.

9. **CONSEQUENTIAL LOSS**  
   Loss that is not a direct result of a breach. Many contracts exclude liability for consequential loss.

10. **CONSIDERATION**  
    What each party gives in exchange in a contract: money, shares, goods, services, or a promise. Essential element of a contract in common law (along with offer, acceptance, and intention).

11. **CONSTRUED / CONSTRUCTION**  
    'Construed' means 'interpreted'. Found in governing law/jurisdiction clauses. *"This Agreement shall be construed in accordance with English Law."*

12. **CONTEMPLATED BY**  
    Means 'within the scope of' this agreement.

13. **COUNTERPARTS**  
    Two identical signed copies of the same agreement, used when parties cannot sign the same document (e.g., different countries).

14. **CREDITORS**  
    People or organisations to whom a business owes money. A **secured creditor** has a charge on the debtor's property.
