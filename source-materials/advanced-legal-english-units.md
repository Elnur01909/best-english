## UNIT 1 — Terms of Art and 'Legalese' in Legal English

### Learning Objectives
- Distinguish between 'terms of art' and 'legalese'
- Understand arguments for and against plain English in legal drafting
- Identify unnecessarily complicated language in contracts

### Key Concepts

**Terms of Art** — technical legal words seen as irreplaceable because they have a judicially defined meaning (e.g., *indemnify*, *consideration*, *novation*).

**Legalese** — non-legal but unnecessarily complicated English words lawyers use when simpler alternatives exist (e.g., *notwithstanding* instead of *despite*, *aforementioned* instead of *previously mentioned*).

### Reading Text
Texas District Judge Lynn Hughes has said:
> *"The common language of the law is not the product of necessity, precedent, convention, or economy, but it is the product of sloth, confusion, hurry, cowardice, ignorance, neglect, and cultural poverty."*

Example of pure legalese (from a Florida house sale contract):
> *"WHEREAS, Seller is desirous of selling the Property as hereinafter defined in Paragraph 2 hereof and Purchaser is desirous of purchasing the Property on the terms and hereinafter set forth…"*

This contains no terms of art — it could simply read: *"The Seller agrees to sell the Property and the Buyer agrees to buy it on the terms set out below."*

### Plain English Drafting Rules
1. Never use a complicated word to appear more educated. Use 'provide', not 'furnish'.
2. Remove Latin from modern contracts.
3. Delete archaic words: 'heretofore', 'hereby', 'foregoing' — use modern equivalents.
4. Do not use lists of synonyms: instead of 'alter, amend, vary, or modify', use one word.
5. Keep sentences short.
6. Use the active voice: *"The Buyer must pay by 9 February"* not *"Payment must be made by the Buyer by 9 February."*

### Vocabulary from Unit 1
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| term of art | a legal word with a judicially defined meaning |
| legalese | unnecessarily complicated legal language |
| watertight contract | a contract drafted so carefully it has no loopholes |
| cross-border contract | a binding agreement between businesses in different jurisdictions |
| school of thought | a set of shared opinions on a debatable subject |
| spell out | to state something very clearly, leaving no doubt |
| plain English | simple, clear language accessible to non-lawyers |

### Exercise
Complete each sentence with a word or phrase from the vocabulary above:
1. A _____ is an agreement drafted so carefully it has no loopholes. *(watertight contract)*
2. A _____ is a set of opinions a group of people share about a debatable subject. *(school of thought)*
3. To _____ the meaning of something is to state it very clearly. *(spell out)*
4. A _____ is a binding agreement between businesses in different jurisdictions. *(cross-border contract)*
5. A _____ is a word used in a contract that has been interpreted in a certain way by a court. *(term of art)*
6. _____ is unnecessarily complicated language lawyers use when simpler words would work. *(Legalese)*

## UNIT 2 — AI and the Law (MIT Technology Review)

### Learning Objectives
- Read and understand a technology article in context of legal English
- Discuss the impact of AI on the legal profession
- Practise vocabulary related to legal technology and automation

### Key Concepts
- **Document discovery tools** — AI trained on millions of documents to identify relevant sources for a legal case
- **COIN (Contract Intelligence)** — JPMorgan's AI that performs in seconds what took legal aides 360,000 hours
- **CaseIQ** — a 'virtual associate' that analyses a brief and suggests improvements
- **22% automation** — McKinsey estimate of how much of a lawyer's job can be automated

### Key Vocabulary
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| augment | to increase or improve something |
| automate | to use technology to perform tasks without human input |
| meticulous | showing great attention to detail; very careful and precise |
| algorithm | a set of rules for a computer to follow to solve a problem |
| redundant | no longer needed (of a worker or job) |
| uptake | the rate at which something is adopted or used |
| paralegal | a person trained in law who assists lawyers but is not qualified as one |
| repository | a large collection of stored information |

### Discussion Points
1. Can AI ever research a subject as meticulously as a human lawyer?
2. McKinsey estimates 22% of a lawyer's job can be automated. Do you welcome or fear this?
3. COIN does 360,000 hours of document review in seconds — is this efficiency worth potential job losses?
4. If AI reduces training opportunities for young lawyers, how can young lawyers 'add value'?

## UNIT 3 — Perfecting Legal Language: Collocation

### Learning Objectives
- Understand what collocations are and why they matter in legal English
- Identify strong and weak collocations
- Practise correct verb + noun and adjective + noun collocations

### What is Collocation?
A collocation is a pair or group of words that naturally go together. Using incorrect collocations makes your English sound unnatural even if the meaning is clear.

**Good:** *to make a decision* / *to adjourn a case* / *to mitigate a loss*  
**Bad:** *to do a decision* / *to suspend a case* / *to reduce a loss* (less natural)

**Strong collocations** — very few alternatives exist (*to mitigate a loss*)  
**Weak collocations** — several options work (*to fulfil / perform / carry out an obligation*)

### Key Legal Collocations Table

| Verb + Noun | Phrasal Verb + Noun | Adjective + Noun | Verb + Adverb |
|-------------|---------------------|------------------|----------------|
| breach a contract | carry out an obligation | a wide range | to fall sharply |
| adjourn a trial | run up a debt | fierce competition | to closely resemble |
| levy a tax | bring up a subject | a final offer | to fail miserably |

### The Snapchat Case — Collocations in Context
*(Frank Brown v Thomas Spiegel & Robert Murphy, 2011–2014)*

Key collocations from the case:
1. To **launch** a new business
2. To **assert** your legal right to something
3. To **instruct** a law firm
4. To **incorporate** a company
5. To **bring** a claim against someone
6. To **deny** an allegation
7. To **settle** a claim out of court
8. To **resolve** a dispute
9. To **disclose** information
10. To **reach** a settlement

### County Court Collocations — Jo Rider v Harry Spencer
Key terms in a County Court claim:
- **outstanding sum** — the full amount still owed
- **due date** — the deadline for payment
- **fill out** a claim form
- **submit** the claim form to the court
- **issue** the claim (court formally starts the process)
- **served** on the defendant (documents officially delivered)
- **stage** of procedure
- **bear** in mind
- **incur** legal fees

## UNIT 4 — Perfecting Legal Language: Prepositions

### Learning Objectives
- Use prepositions correctly in legal English
- Understand common prepositional phrases in contracts
- Avoid common preposition errors made by non-native English lawyers

### Key Prepositional Phrases in Legal English

| Phrase | Meaning / Usage |
|--------|-----------------|
| in accordance with | following the rules of; as required by |
| in respect of | about; concerning; with reference to |
| in the event of/that | if something happens |
| subject to | conditional upon; dependent on |
| pursuant to | in accordance with; as a result of |
| on behalf of | representing; acting for |
| by virtue of | because of; as a result of |
| in lieu of | instead of |
| without prejudice | without admitting liability (used in settlement negotiations) |
| notwithstanding | despite; in spite of |

### Common Errors to Avoid
- ❌ *in accordance of* → ✓ *in accordance with*
- ❌ *in respect to* → ✓ *in respect of*
- ❌ *pursuant of* → ✓ *pursuant to*
- ❌ *on behalf for* → ✓ *on behalf of*
- ❌ *by virtue to* → ✓ *by virtue of*

### Practice Sentences
1. "This Agreement shall be interpreted _______ English law." *(in accordance with)*
2. "The Company accepts liability _______ any damage caused during delivery." *(in respect of)*
3. "Either party may terminate this Agreement _______ a material breach." *(in the event of)*
4. "The payment shall be made _______ the provisions of clause 5." *(pursuant to)*
5. "The solicitor signed the document _______ his client." *(on behalf of)*

## UNIT 5 — Reading Real Contracts: Structure and Interpretation

### Learning Objectives
- Understand the standard structure of a commercial contract
- Interpret definitions, conditions and operative clauses
- Identify boilerplate clauses and understand their function

### Standard Contract Structure

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Parties | Identifies who has entered the agreement |
| Recitals (WHEREAS clauses) | Background context for the agreement |
| Definitions | Defines capitalised terms used throughout |
| Operative Clauses | The actual obligations of each party |
| Conditions Precedent | What must happen before obligations arise |
| Representations & Warranties | Promises about present facts |
| Indemnities | Who pays if something goes wrong |
| Limitation of Liability | Caps on the amount of damages |
| Termination | When and how the contract can be ended |
| Boilerplate / General | Force majeure, governing law, notices, counterparts, entire agreement |

### Key Boilerplate Clauses

**Entire Agreement Clause**  
States that the written contract is the complete agreement — no prior oral agreements are incorporated. Prevents parties from claiming that other things were agreed.

**Force Majeure Clause**  
Excuses a party from performance if an extraordinary and unforeseeable event prevents it (earthquake, war, pandemic). Note: the clause must specifically list or describe qualifying events.

**Governing Law Clause**  
Specifies which country's law governs the contract. *"This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of England and Wales."*

**Jurisdiction Clause**  
Specifies which court has authority to hear disputes. Can be exclusive (only one court) or non-exclusive.

**Notice Clause**  
Specifies how formal notices must be given (e.g., by registered post or email) and when they are deemed received.

**Severability Clause**  
If one clause is found to be invalid, the rest of the contract remains in force.

### Key Vocabulary
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| recitals | introductory background paragraphs (WHEREAS clauses) |
| operative clause | a clause setting out actual contractual obligations |
| representation | a statement of present fact made to induce someone to enter a contract |
| warranty | a contractual promise that certain facts are true |
| indemnity | an obligation to compensate the other party for a specific loss |
| severability | the ability of the rest of a contract to survive if one clause is invalid |
| entire agreement clause | a clause stating that the written contract is the complete agreement |
| force majeure | extraordinary and unforeseeable event preventing performance |
