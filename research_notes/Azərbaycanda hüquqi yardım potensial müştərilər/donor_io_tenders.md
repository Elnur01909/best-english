# Donor / International-Organisation Tenders and Calls for English-Language Legal Expertise in Azerbaijan (2025–2026, plus recurring buyers)

Research date: 8 November 2026. Scope: procurement notices, individual-consultant (IC) calls, RFPs and expert rosters from UN agencies, development banks, EU/CoE/bilateral donors, INGOs and embassies that buy **legal expertise delivered in Azerbaijan in English**.

**Verification caveat (read first).** The research sandbox blocked direct page fetches to every procurement portal (ungm.org, undp.org, azerbaijan.un.org, ebrd.com, adb.org, worldbank.org, coe.int, gov.uk, state.gov, developmentaid.org, impactpool.org, busy.az); a request to allow those hosts was declined, and the shared web-search budget ran out before a last confirmation round. Consequently every URL below was surfaced by the search engine and every fact quoted was taken from the search engine's own retrieval of that page's content — none was invented — but **no page was opened by hand**. Where a deadline or reference number did not appear in the retrieved content it is marked "not retrieved" rather than guessed. Items marked *(aggregator)* were seen on DevelopmentAid/Impactpool/UNtalent mirrors rather than the issuer's own site.

## Key Question 0 — Consolidated list of concrete opportunities / organisations (objective)

### Takeaway
Thirteen concrete notices or recurring buyers were identified. Only two look plausibly open as of November 2026 (EBRD PR 020959 "Technical Support for Establishment of Compliance Function (ASCO)", tagged *Law* sector, and the ILO UNGM call 313724 for an MLC-2006 legal gap analysis for Azerbaijan); the rest are closed 2022–2026 notices that show which organisations repeatedly buy English-language legal work in Azerbaijan (UNICEF, UNDP, Council of Europe Baku, EBRD, World Bank/Ministry of Justice, IOM, ILO). UNHCR — historically the biggest buyer of outsourced legal services in Azerbaijan — closed its office in August 2025 and is no longer a prospect; USAID's legal basis in Azerbaijan ended in 2024.

### Inferences
- The only plausibly live items are #2 (ILO/UNGM) and #3 (EBRD PR 020959); both deadlines must be checked on the issuer's portal before any outreach.
- UNICEF (#1), Council of Europe Baku (#9–#12) and UNDP (#7–#8) are *repeat* buyers of national legal drafting / gap-analysis / legal-aid-review expertise and are the best direct-outreach prospects; CoE in particular issues framework contracts with "pools" of national consultants, which a lawyer can join without a formal tender win.
- English is the default working language for the international-consultant lots (#2, #7, #8, #9), while national lots (#1, #4, #11, #12, #14) require English plus Azerbaijani (and often Russian).
- UNHCR (#6) is no longer a prospect, but the three NGOs/law firms that held its 2024 legal-services contracts are themselves prospects for sub-contracting or referral (names not retrieved).

### Gaps
- Deadlines for #2, #3, #4, #5, #12, #14 were not visible in retrieved content; the UNGM, ECEPP and rm.coe.int pages must be opened directly to confirm.
- Contract-award pages (e.g., [UNGM ContractAward 155077](https://www.ungm.org/Public/ContractAward/155077), [UNGM ContractAward 159059](https://www.ungm.org/Public/ContractAward/159059)) appeared in results but their content (which firm won which legal-services contract) was not retrieved.
- A reported EBRD GEFF Azerbaijan facility-consultant notice requiring a "Legal Team Leader" (late-2025) was mentioned in search output without a retrievable URL, so it is excluded.

## Key Question 4 — INGOs / legal networks (Open Society, ABA ROLI, IDLO, ICJ, TI, Freedom House, PILnet, TrustLaw, DLA Piper New Perspectives)

### Takeaway
No active Azerbaijan-specific programme from PILnet, ABA ROLI, IDLO, TrustLaw or DLA Piper was found; the only INGO legal-consultant vacancy located is an ICJ legal-researcher call from 2018. ## Key Question 5 — Embassies publishing English-speaking lawyer lists in Azerbaijan, and how to get listed

### Takeaway
The US Embassy Baku posts a list of attorneys (including family-law specialists), governed by State Department rules requiring triennial updates and based on information the lawyers themselves supply; the British Embassy keeps an English-speaking lawyer list available on request (not online); the Israeli Embassy publishes a lawyer list on its website. No Canadian, Australian, German or Dutch published lists were found (Australia has no resident embassy).

### Cited Findings
- State Department: "The U.S. Embassy in Baku, Azerbaijan, posts a list of attorneys, including those who specialize in family law… provided as a courtesy service only and does not constitute an endorsement" — [travel.state.gov IPCA Azerbaijan](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/International-Parental-Child-Abduction/International-Parental-Child-Abduction-Country-Information/Azerbaijan.html).
